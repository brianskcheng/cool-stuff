Gravity as a designer

A chain hanging between two points finds the one shape in which every link is in pure tension. Turn that shape upside down and every link is in pure compression: the ideal arch. Robert Hooke hid the idea in a Latin anagram in 1675.

Antoni Gaudí designed with webs of chains and little sacks of shot hung from a ceiling board, then photographed them and flipped the photographs. Heinz Isler hung wet fabric, let it freeze, and inverted it to get concrete shells only a few centimetres thick. Frei Otto, Felix Candela and the Block Research Group all work in the same family.

What you are playing with. The cloth is a net of about 2,500 nodes and 10,000 tension-only links, simulated live with extended position-based dynamics (XPBD). The links stretch in proportion to their force. Flip mirrors the settled net, so tension becomes compression. Drag a support in either state and the form re-finds its equilibrium.

The numbers. Stress is a virial average over each node under self-weight only (24 kN/m³ concrete), with the support zones excluded. Under self-weight, membrane stress does not depend on thickness. What limits real thin shells is buckling, plus snow, wind and asymmetric loads, and none of those are modelled here. Treat it as a sketchbook, not a design tool.
