A museum in four rooms

The Museum of Other Hands

Every room here is glued to itself. Walk out through one wall and you come back in through the opposite one. In some rooms the glue has a twist, and you come back as your own reflection. The doors only open for visitors who notice.

How it works

The copies are not reflections. They are the room.

Each room is one square of floor. Each pair of opposite walls is either a real wall, glued straight across, or glued with a half-twist. Glue one pair straight and you get a cylinder. Glue both and you get a torus. Twist one pair for a Möbius band, or twist one of two glued pairs for a Klein bottle.

What you walk around in is the universal cover: the whole plane, tiled by copies of the square, one for each deck transformation. Every deck transformation here has the form (x, z) ↦ (±x + a, ±z + b), and a copy is mirrored exactly when the two signs differ. The renderer draws the room once and instances it about eighty times, one instance per deck transformation near you. Lighting is computed in each copy's own frame.

You never flip. The camera is always an ordinary right-handed camera. What changes is which copy of the room you are standing in, and in half of them the room is the mirror image of the one you started in. So a left-hand print, seen from a mirrored copy, is a right-hand print. An L-shaped key you pick up in one copy is a J to the room in the next.

All four surfaces are flat: their copies tile the plane without stretching. Twist both pairs and you get the projective plane, which cannot be flat. That is why the free walk won't offer it.
