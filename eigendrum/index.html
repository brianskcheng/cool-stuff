Can you hear the shape of a drum?

Draw any shape. In about a second this page solves the wave equation on it and finds its 64 lowest modes of vibration. Then you can play it, watch it ring, and see how much of the shape the sound gives away.

Headphones help. Everything is computed live, in your browser.

How Eigendrum works

A drumhead is a stretched membrane fixed at its rim. Strike it and it vibrates as a sum of modes: standing waves, each with its own frequency. The mode shapes and pitches depend only on the outline. Mark Kac asked in 1966 whether the reverse is true: whether the pitches pin down the outline.

The solver

Your outline is sampled on a grid of about 7,000 points. The rim boundary condition is placed at the exact distance from each grid point to the curve, not snapped to the nearest point. The Laplacian becomes a sparse matrix, which is factored once with an envelope Cholesky decomposition. A shift-and-invert block Lanczos iteration then pulls out the 64 lowest eigenvalues and eigenvectors. It all runs in a Web Worker, in plain JavaScript. On a square it reproduces the exact discrete eigenvalues to ten digits; on a circle it is within 0.1% of the Bessel zeros.

The sound

Each mode is a damped resonator in an AudioWorklet. A strike at a point excites mode k in proportion to the mode’s height there, so striking near the rim sounds brighter than striking the middle, just as on a real drum. Mallet hardness filters the high modes, and each mode’s loudness is weighted by how much air it pushes. Rubbing feeds friction noise into the modes under your finger.

What the spectrum gives away

Weyl’s law says the number of modes below frequency f grows like (area) × f², minus a correction proportional to the rim length. The panel fits those two terms to your drum’s 64 frequencies and recovers its area and perimeter from the sound alone.

What it doesn’t

In 1992 Gordon, Webb and Wolpert found two different shapes with identical spectra, each built from seven half-squares glued according to the Fano plane. Try Two drums, one voice. The page solves both drums on lattice-aligned grids, so even the discrete problems are exactly isospectral: all 64 frequencies match to round-off.

Tabla tuning

With the Mass tool you can load the head unevenly. Harmonize uses the derivative of each eigenvalue with respect to the density at every point (∂λ/∂ρ = −λφ²) to move mass until the overtones fall on the harmonic series. C. V. Raman studied exactly this effect in the tabla.
